## Precise new executable self-modification

The native world still uses the **unchanged** Rust `World::with_strict_capacity(16,180,300)`, one original PID1 per world with **6,000 real private credits**, no resets, no second process, no host source changes, and 91 original CIL instructions. All 24 hidden service stages are driven by the same original process in each arm. The original paid `Write11` task guess precedes external `SetCell20`, followed by original paid `Read20`. An earned goal is stored by original `Write34`, later read by `Read34`, and the relative early-goal delta determines a paid `PatchConst` to **executable stride PC46**.

The added P4F0 structural mechanism is specifically **not per-success polling**:

1. After the second real task success, the same PID1 pays `PatchNext` at PC82 to change the **real executable next edge of its original failure-count `Write` node PC12 from 13 to 83**. This arms a new failure-only detector. This edit is an original native CIL opcode and is charged, not an external modification.
2. After a future task guess fails, the original PID first pays to increment failure count in actual data cell33. Only the modified edge now sends it into the detector. A failure count of two in that task causes the original process to pay `PatchConst PC88`, setting PC46 to the **researcher-authored fallback stride1**. Every check, branch, patch and register restoration costs original private compute.
3. The causal native control uses **the same source** and `with_native_patchnext_disabled()`: it still pays PC82 and still permits the original paid PC45 policy calibration, but the failure node edge is not changed. The contrast therefore isolates the effect of arming this monitor, rather than disabling all self-modification or giving a counterfactual goal oracle.
4. The fallback does **not** preserve a priced old-policy recovery channel. It may persist after a short shock is over. The same original PID never sees future goals and cannot distinguish persistence versus transience from a single surprise.

## Falsification and information limits

- **Structural result:** actor-paid `PatchNext` genuinely changes when its own program executes environmental monitoring code, and a second paid source `PatchConst` can be causally gated by its actual failures. A monitor that sleeps during normal success is feasible under unchanged original CL0 physics.
- **Finite success:** on alternate and scrambled families this new executable condition saves task service and source-paid compute compared with suppressing the same detector in source-identical actors.
- **Robustness failure:** on the **heldout transient shock** family it spends **1,532 additional original CIL instructions**, incurs **48 extra failed service attempts**, and cannot repair the obsolete fallback after the environment returns to its prior pattern. Across the six selected families, the total is **170 net extra paid instructions**, not a survival or generalization win. The stable family has zero *paired detector* cost because both on/off arms pay to arm PC82 and none thereafter performs failure checks; this is **not** proof that P4F0 is absolutely free compared with the shorter P3 source.
- **No future identifiability:** the actor sees only the charged event/feedback history; it cannot know from one observed mismatch whether a new regime will persist.
- **Nonclaims:** threshold2, fallback stride1, 24-stage horizon, task alphabet and schedule families were supplied by researchers. There is no discovered algorithm or general fitness improvement, no integrated I6U structural repair in this specific actor, no within-world evolution across the 110 separate fresh PIDs, and no CL-1B completion.
