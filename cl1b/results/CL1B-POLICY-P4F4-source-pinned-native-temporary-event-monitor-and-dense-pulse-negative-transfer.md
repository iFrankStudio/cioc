## Prospective protocol and mechanism

Frozen before native execution by [study commit 8e261ff8](https://github.com/iFrankStudio/cioc/commit/8e261ff8bcc45b1539580b2d9c3a60493a37ec59): `cl1b/studies/CL1B-POLICY-P4F4-frozen-actor-paid-temporary-success-monitor-326-world-v1.json`, protocol Markdown. Original-native producer and independent replayer were then committed in [b693ff62](https://github.com/iFrankStudio/cioc/commit/b693ff626e81429cfdf80f652fecfc91761a6968) at the exact source the above CI tested.

Original unchanged Rust `World::with_strict_capacity(16,180,300)`, private 6,000 compute credits per independent original PID1, no PID2, 24 hidden actual task goal stages. P4F4 starts with **142 original CIL source nodes**; the comparison P4F3 has 132. Each of **108 frozen 24-goal sequences** has three native worlds: identical F4 original source with all actor-paid `PatchNext` effects enabled vs disabled (opcode charges retained), and a separate original F3 source comparator with edits enabled. Another two F4 worlds test inverted feedback and forced-zero feedback. **324 true-feedback worlds + 2 adversarial = 326.** The original 72 previously seen P4F3 cases are reused; **36 genuinely new frozen holdouts** consist of sparse one-stage pulses, dense repeated two-stage pulses, delayed reversion and multiple stride shifts, with 9 variants per family.

F4 pays once to hook executable original failed-task `Write PC12.next` to PC137 by `PatchNext PC82`, and pays once to hook successful-stage `Write PC34.next` to **cheap PC132** via `PatchNext PC131`. Normal successive successes pay **two CIL instructions** to `Write37` the most recent task success without running F3's full revalidation logic. If original paid `Write11` fails and PID receives feedback from paid `Read20`, the original failure path pays `PatchNext PC139` to temporarily redirect `PC34.next` to the expensive success handler PC106, then pays `Read33` to restore its failure counter. Following eventual truthful success, original PID pays `Read37`, computes relative goal change mod3, conditionally pays `PatchConst PC122` to edit executable stride PC46, and pays `PatchNext PC136` to restore `PC34.next` to cheap PC132 for future tasks; original Write37 and stage-register restoration remain paid. Original source, source edges PC12/PC34, executable PC46, four registers, cells, PID compute credit and external feedback are observed and separately reconstructed **after each original native paid instruction**. **No host post-load source changes or free future-goal access.**

## Functional equivalence as a separate result

A secondary analysis of `cl1b_policy_p4f4_native_326_worlds.json` confirms **all 108/108 F4-on and F3-on task-service sequences match exactly** when comparing every (stage, output guess, actual truth, delivered feedback) in order, *and* all 108/108 sequences of (source PatchConst PC, desired executable stride) match exactly. Thus **all F4/F3 task-probe counts agree and all actual service behaviors and computed executable search strides are observationally identical** on this selected finite corpus; the F4/F3 compute-cost delta is the difference in how expensively the author-designed monitoring policy is maintained, not less service work or superior task answers. This does *not* prove equivalence in untested environments. From native summary, total original paid instructions: **F4-on 140,523; F3-on 144,531; F4-off 133,113**, plus both adversarial controls = **419,679**.

All **324 truthful worlds** complete 24 real stages and halt with truthful goal completion. In the F4 **inverted-feedback** control, the original actor **claims 24 successes but only 11 actual task service outputs are correct** (1,444 charged instructions); the **forced-zero feedback** control halts with an actor failure claim (68 charged original instructions, 1 incidental correct service). This demonstrates the actor can still be deceived by externally supplied reward bits. Original native one-PID worlds are *not* connected as one lineage.
