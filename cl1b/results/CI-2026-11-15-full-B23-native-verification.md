## New B22–B23 causal lesson

The **native B22** experiment shows that one CIL founder can construct, modify and launch a new resource-relay executable using real paid graph primitives. It has only one externally loaded process, creates its second process internally, transfers child initial credits and then receives actual native resource transfer.

The **native B23** test and independent source-shaped observer each compare 18 worlds: fixed equal initial compute **29**, the same environmental supply total **8** under two pulse schedules `[3,5]` and `[4,4]`, and genuine internal service writes only. The founder-assembled relay yields **one** valid service under either schedule; a simpler **5-opcode** solo request3 program yields **two**. The B22 parent founder contains35 source nodes and ends using43 storage units vs seven for that solo baseline. The no-transfer ablation yields zero service. Both positive policies are fully **researcher-authored**; no participant chose its own better algorithm.

**Strong negative conclusion:** more native graph construction / spawned processes do not automatically improve self-maintenance under equal initial compute and resource flux. Added organization may impose a substantial compute/storage cost. The observed two-to-one efficiency difference is not evidence of a self-adaptive solo actor, only an engineering counterexample to complexity-as-fitness.
