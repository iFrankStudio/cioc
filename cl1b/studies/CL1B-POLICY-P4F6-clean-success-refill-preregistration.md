# CL1B-POLICY-P4F6 — preregistered replenishable online source-edit budget

**Frozen before original P4F6 Rust native execution.** Follows independently verified [P4F5 434-world SUCCESS](https://github.com/iFrankStudio/cioc/actions/runs/44860926121); P4F5's permanent maximum one online PC122 source edit is the baseline.

F6 uses the **exact original CL0 limits of 16 distinct data cells, 180 source slots, 300 occupied units and 6,000 original PID1 private credits**. The F6 program occupies **all 180 source slots**—no spare source capacity. It begins with the inherited actor-paid P4F5 edit budget (cell38) and adds a paid clean-success streak counter (cell39). After a paid online PC122 source change, original PID1 pays to modify its own executable cheap-success `PC133.next` from 62→156 by `PatchNext PC155`. For each **genuinely successful stage**, it pays to read real prior failures from cell33 and either resets streak cell39 on a stage with failures, or increments cell39 on a clean success. **Four consecutive clean stage successes** pay to reset cell39 and cell38 to zero, then PID1 pays `PatchNext PC177` to undo monitoring by rewiring `PC133.next=62`, restoring original task-stage register with paid `Read32`. It may later pay for a new online PC122 source edit after new task failure. All instructions/edits/feedback observations are charged. This is an **author-designed hard threshold**, not endogenous utility estimation.

**Important negative pilot, reused known cases only:** source-level pre-native screen on 144 P4F5-known goal sequences shows F6 **12,455 extra charged instructions** relative to distinct F5 program but **114 fewer task probes**, despite all truthful episodes completing. This supports a falsifiable risk of monitor cost exceeding its benefit. These cases are **not** prospective independent holdouts and must not be described as such.

Three separate original Rust native worlds per all 180 sequences (F6 actor edits active, identical F6 source PatchNext effects suppressed, F5 actor edits active); plus two adversarial feedback controls: **542 original worlds total**.
