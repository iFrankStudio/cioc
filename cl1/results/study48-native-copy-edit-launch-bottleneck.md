Even under the more expansive Study46 24-choice stochastic graph grammar, independent successful native source copying and graph editing events **never co-occurred on the same future-launched source graph** in the entire frozen 128-world cohort. Therefore no full native assembly sequence comparable to the scripted Study47 positive exists in the observed Study44/46 worlds.

**Precise limitation:** Study48 counts source-node indexes >0 rather than literal source opcode type or its intrinsic fitness. Its structural graph-closure and prelaunch assembly indicators are retrospective explanatory gates, not organism selection. Zero observed copy→edit→launch does **not** prove those operations are impossible in the CIL VM; Study47 deliberately demonstrates their native feasibility with an authored program. The missing event chain is an ecological *opportunity and coordination bottleneck*, not missing instructions or an unsupported kernel.

## Decision for next independent research

Stop treating `GraphLaunch` frequency or GraphNew loops as reproduction. For future prospectively registered studies, test **generic coordination of native graph assembly events** without fixed human authored successful sequences, preferably by varying neutral initial code accessibility and environment rather than embedding Write repair/proliferation targets. Primary endpoints must preserve native actor/graph ancestry, chronologically closed executable edge topology, functioning child shared output, and later endogenous maintenance after real lesions (with no targeted host rescue). Use independent new seeds and source-pinned SQLite.

**CL-1 OPEN.** Demonstrated: actor-native self-code editing and externally supported child launching. Not demonstrated: stochastic native functional child assembly, endogenous self-repair or autonomous cyberlife.
